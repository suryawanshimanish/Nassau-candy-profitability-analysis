# Executive Summary — Nassau Candy Product Profitability

**Audience:** Government / public-sector stakeholders  
**Prepared:** 14 November 2026  
**Reporting period:** 2 January 2024 – 31 December 2025

> **Bottom line.** The portfolio is profitable but fragile. It earned **$93,442.80** in gross profit on **$141,783.63** in sales (**65.91%** weighted gross margin), yet five chocolate products provide **95.06%** of profit. The margin problem is concentrated in a small cost-heavy tail, not in geography or short-term volatility.

## What the evidence says

- **High sales are not the same as high profitability.** Lickable Wallpaper contributes 5.54% of revenue at only 50.00% margin. Triple Dazzle Caramel leads core-bar revenue but has a below-portfolio margin.
- **Kazookles is an acute intervention candidate.** Cost absorbs 92.31% of sales, leaving 7.69% margin and $92.75 gross profit.
- **Concentration is the main resilience risk.** Only 5 of 15 products are needed to cross 80% of profit; the top five deliver 95.06%. The brief's mapping links the whole chocolate range to two factories.
- **Geography is not the cause.** Regional margins differ by 0.96 pp. California, New York and Texas represent 40.11% of revenue—volume concentration, not proof of congestion.

## Decisions recommended

1. Protect supply and margin on the five core chocolate products; require continuity plans and avoid broad volume-led discounting.
2. Commission a 60–90 day repricing and supplier-cost test for Lickable Wallpaper, with elasticity and cannibalization measured before national rollout.
3. Require an immediate Kazookles cost teardown; if a viable 60% margin cannot be demonstrated, conduct a time-bound discontinuation review.
4. Pilot the two highest-margin low-volume products and review the nine-product micro-tail only after adding handling, freight, returns and shelf-space costs.
5. Correct source-system dates before any logistics or public reporting use: all Order ID years conflict with order dates, and implied ship lags are 904–1642 days.

## Public-sector interpretation

These findings can inform supply-chain resilience, small-supplier diversification and evidence standards for distributor reporting. They **cannot** support conclusions about market power, consumer harm, regulatory compliance or actual delivery reliability. The portfolio HHI is an internal dependency measure; no external market-share data are present, and the shipment dates are unusable.
